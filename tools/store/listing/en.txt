@@Description
Turn your e-books and documents into clean, readable web pages - right on your PC, no account needed.

doc-html-translate converts EPUB, PDF, MOBI, AZW3, FB2, RTF, TXT, Markdown, HTML, comic archives
(CBZ, CBR, CB7, CBT) and images into clean local HTML with generated navigation and a real multi-level
table of contents. Open the result in any browser and read - or hand it straight to your browser's own
page translation for a free, no-key translation into any language.

A built-in reader adds light / sepia / dark / night themes, text-size and font controls, and remembers
where you stopped. Convert to one long single page or keep chapters with a table of contents; re-opening
an already-converted book is instant.

Optional on-image OCR recognizes text inside pictures and scanned pages (English bundled, more languages
on demand) and can translate everything - either through the Google Cloud Translation API or a fully
local Ollama model, so nothing has to leave your machine.

The interface speaks 13 languages: English, Russian, Ukrainian, German, Italian, Spanish, French,
Portuguese, Arabic, Hindi, Bengali, Urdu and Chinese.

Big PDFs stay fast, scanned pages are handled, and a wide range of text encodings just work. The package
ships both a command-line tool (doc-html-translate) and a small GUI launcher (doc-html-ui). pdftotext is
bundled inside the binary; MOBI and AZW3 need Calibre installed (non-DRM files only).

No account, no ads, no telemetry. Open source: https://github.com/SerZhyAle/doc-html-translate

@@ReleaseNotes
- Text recognized on a picture now covers the lettering it replaces. Until now the replacement was painted on a patch shaped like the words themselves, so the original stayed visible around it and both layers were readable at once.
- A screenshot, a form or a list is no longer buried under one giant patch. A plate that spans most of the picture is split back into the lines it was built from, so the page keeps its own layout and nothing that was read is lost.
- If you do not choose an OCR language, the app now reads the writing system on the page. Where it has the data it recognizes the page in that language; where it does not it adds no text at all and names the language pack to install - instead of covering a Russian screenshot in transliterated nonsense.
- Lettering on saturated artwork, on a halftone screen, or set as large display type is recognized where earlier versions found nothing at all.
- Comics and scans: every page has its own anchor and a page jump box, so you can link to page 30 instead of dragging the scrollbar.
- A new button hides the recognized-text layer, so you can look at the artwork underneath and bring the text back.
- A PDF with neither a text layer nor an extractable page image now says exactly that, instead of claiming OCR was switched off.
